# Shortcut substitutions

The shortcut version is the same dish with less work. It should cut real
hands-on time by buying work that the store already did (chopping, cooking,
soaking, simmering), not just make the recipe a bit shorter.

> **Status: draft, waiting for Rachel's review.** The conversion amounts below
> are common kitchen rules of thumb, not measured. Treat them as starting points
> until they've been checked.

## How to build a shortcut version

1. Go through the from-scratch ingredient list one line at a time and ask:
   *does the store sell this already prepped?* (chopped, cooked, shredded,
   blended, soaked, sauced).
2. Make every swap that saves real time **and** doesn't hurt the dish (see
   "When not to swap" below).
3. **Convert the amount, don't just copy it.** Dried herbs, garlic paste, canned
   beans and spice packets are not 1:1 with the fresh versions.
4. **Re-balance the salt.** Canned goods, broth, seasoning packets, jarred
   sauces and rotisserie chicken all bring salt. Cut the added salt and say
   "season to taste at the end".
5. Rewrite the steps. If a step still says "chop", "dice", "mince", "soak" or
   "simmer for an hour", the shortcut missed something.
6. Recalculate the time. A good shortcut usually saves at least 30% of the
   scratch time. If it saves less than about 5 minutes, look again.

## Prepped produce (skip the cutting board)

| Scratch | Shortcut | Amount | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chopped onion | Fresh pre-diced onion (produce cooler) or frozen diced onion | 1 medium onion ≈ 1 cup diced | Frozen onion is soft and watery, so use it only in cooked dishes. Sauté it a little longer to drive off the water. |
| Chopped onion + tomato + cilantro (+ jalapeño) | Fresh pico de gallo (produce or deli) | Swap cup for cup | Brings its own salt and lime. It replaces several lines and a whole chopping step. |
| Diced onion, carrot, celery | Pre-cut mirepoix / "soup starter" mix | Swap cup for cup | |
| Minced garlic | Jarred minced garlic or garlic paste tube | ½ tsp jarred ≈ 1 clove | Milder and a bit sour. Add a little extra, and don't use it raw (for example in a garlic-forward dressing). |
| Grated fresh ginger | Ginger paste tube | 1 tsp paste ≈ 1 tsp grated | Close enough for stir-fries and curries. |
| Shredded lettuce or cabbage | Bagged shredded lettuce, coleslaw mix, salad kit | Swap cup for cup | |
| Sliced mushrooms, peppers, zucchini | Pre-sliced fresh, or frozen pepper-and-onion strips | Swap cup for cup | Frozen ones release water. Don't add them to a pan that's supposed to brown. |
| Fresh spinach to cook down | Frozen chopped spinach | 10 oz frozen ≈ 1 lb fresh, cooked | Thaw it and squeeze it dry, or the dish goes watery. |
| Peeled, cubed squash or sweet potato | Pre-cubed fresh or frozen | Swap by weight | Frozen cubes roast softer and brown less. |
| Riced or spiralized vegetables | Bagged/frozen cauliflower rice, zucchini noodles | Swap by weight | |
| Fresh corn, peas, green beans | Frozen | Swap cup for cup | Usually just as good cooked. Canned is softer and saltier. |

## Herbs and spices (the amounts change)

| Scratch | Shortcut | Amount | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh leafy herbs, cooked (thyme, oregano, rosemary, sage) | Dried | **1 tsp dried ≈ 1 tbsp fresh** (1:3) | Add dried herbs early so they soften and bloom. Rosemary needles stay sharp, so crush them. |
| Fresh soft herbs, cooked (basil, parsley, dill) | Dried | 1:3, as above | A weaker swap. Dried parsley and basil taste of very little. |
| Fresh herbs used **raw or as a garnish** (cilantro on tacos, basil on pizza) | **Don't use dried.** Use herb paste tubes or freeze-dried herbs, or just leave it off | Freeze-dried is usually about 1:1; check the jar | Dried cilantro tastes like nothing. Keep these fresh and buy a bunch, since chopping cilantro takes about 30 seconds. |
| Fresh garlic | Garlic powder | ⅛ tsp powder ≈ 1 clove | No browned-garlic flavor. Fine in rubs, sauces and soups. |
| Onion for flavor only (in a rub or sauce) | Onion powder | 1 tbsp ≈ 1 medium onion | Flavor only, with none of the bulk or sweetness. Only swap it when the onion isn't really a vegetable in the dish. |
| Fresh ginger | Ground ginger | ¼ tsp ground ≈ 1 tbsp grated | Tastes different (warmer, less zingy). Fine in braises; skip it in stir-fries and use the paste instead. |
| Several separate spices (chili powder, cumin, garlic, oregano, paprika) | Seasoning packet (taco, fajita, chili, ranch) | 1 packet (about 1 oz, 2–3 tbsp) per lb of meat | Packets are salty. Drop the recipe's added salt and taste at the end. |
| Whole spices toasted and ground | Ground spices | About ¾ the volume of whole | Toast the ground spice in the oil for 30 seconds instead. It scorches fast. |
| Fresh lemon or lime juice in a cooked dish | Bottled juice | Swap tsp for tsp | Fine cooked. Use fresh for anything raw (guacamole, dressings, finishing). |

## Canned, jarred and packaged (big time savers)

These cut the most time, often 30 minutes to overnight.

| Scratch | Shortcut | Amount | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dried beans, soaked and cooked (overnight soak + 1–2 h) | Canned beans | 1 can (15 oz) ≈ 1½ cups drained ≈ ½ cup dried | Rinse them to remove roughly a third of the salt. A huge win: always swap unless beans are the star and the cook wants to cook them. |
| Beans cooked and mashed for refried beans | Canned refried beans | 1 can (16 oz) ≈ 1¾ cups | |
| Homemade stock (hours) | Carton broth, or bouillon base (Better Than Bouillon type) | 1 tsp base + 1 cup water ≈ 1 cup stock | Choose low-sodium and salt at the end. Less body than homemade, so for a pan sauce, reduce it a bit longer. |
| Fresh tomatoes in a **cooked** sauce or braise | Canned diced, crushed or fire-roasted | 1 can (14.5 oz) ≈ 1¾ cups ≈ 3 medium tomatoes | Canned is often better than winter tomatoes. Fire-roasted adds smokiness. Not for raw uses (salsa fresca, salads). |
| Tomato sauce or marinara from scratch | Jarred marinara | Swap cup for cup | Taste for sweetness; some jars are sugary. |
| Sauce made from scratch (enchilada, curry, teriyaki, tinga, BBQ, pesto, Alfredo) | Jarred or refrigerated version of the same sauce | Swap cup for cup, matched to the recipe's sauce amount | The sauce sets the dish's flavor, so pick one close to the original. Often the single biggest time saver. |
| Roasting and peeling peppers | Jarred roasted red peppers | 1 large pepper ≈ ½ cup jarred | Drain and pat dry. |
| Cooking chicken to shred | Rotisserie chicken | 1 chicken ≈ 3–4 cups shredded meat | Salty and pre-seasoned. Cut the salt. |
| Peeling and deveining shrimp | Frozen peeled, deveined shrimp | Swap by weight | Thaw them in cold water for 10 minutes. |
| Cooking rice or grains (20–45 min) | Microwave pouch or frozen rice | 1 pouch ≈ 2 cups cooked | |
| Pie crust, pizza dough, biscuits | Refrigerated or frozen | | |
| Grating or crumbling cheese | Pre-shredded or pre-crumbled | Swap cup for cup | Pre-shredded has anti-caking starch and melts less smoothly. Grate your own for smooth cheese sauces and queso. |
| Caramelized onions (40+ min) | No good shortcut | | Cook them on Sunday (see prepAhead), or pick a different dish for the weeknight. Don't pretend 5 minutes is enough. |

## When not to swap

- **The ingredient is the point.** Fresh mozzarella in a caprese, fresh herbs in
  pesto, homemade stock in a broth-forward soup. Keep it, and save time
  elsewhere.
- **Raw or garnish uses.** Frozen, canned and dried versions are for cooking.
- **Baking.** Leave flour, leavening, fat and sugar ratios alone.
- **It doesn't save real time.** Swapping one whole avocado for a container of
  guacamole only makes sense if the recipe needs it mashed.
- **Kid texture.** Frozen or canned vegetables are softer, which is often a plus
  for the 2-year-old and sometimes a minus for the 6-year-old. Mention it in
  kidTip when it matters.

## Worked example: Chicken Tinga Tostadas

The imported shortcut swapped only the chicken (rotisserie) and the tomatoes
(canned). It still sliced the onion, chopped the chipotle and cilantro, and
built the sauce from parts. A real shortcut:

| Scratch line | Shortcut line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked chicken breast, shredded | Rotisserie chicken, shredded | No cooking or shredding by fork |
| Tomatoes, onion, chipotle, oil, simmered into tinga sauce | Jarred chipotle or tinga simmer sauce, about ⅓ cup per serving | The sauce is the slow part. A jar replaces four lines and two steps. |
| White onion, sliced, for topping | Pico de gallo, 2 tbsp | Replaces the onion and most of the cilantro, so there's no chopping |
| Cilantro, chopped | Keep a little fresh, or leave it out since the pico has it | A garnish herb, so don't use dried |
| Queso fresco, crumbled | Pre-crumbled queso fresco or cotija | |
| Lettuce | Bagged shredded lettuce | |
| Refried beans | Canned refried beans | |
| Salt | Leave it out; season to taste | The jarred sauce and rotisserie chicken are already salty |

Steps: warm the chicken in the sauce (5 min); microwave the beans; spread them
on the tostadas; top them. About 10 minutes, compared with 25 from scratch.

## Checklist before saving a shortcut version

- [ ] No step still asks for chopping, mincing, soaking or long simmering that
      the store could have done.
- [ ] Every swapped herb, spice, garlic and bean amount is converted, not copied.
- [ ] Added salt is reduced where salty products came in.
- [ ] Garnish and raw ingredients stayed fresh.
- [ ] The time is honestly lower, and the dish is still recognizably the same.
